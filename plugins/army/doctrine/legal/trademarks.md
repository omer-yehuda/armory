# Trademarks & naming

Goal: the product name, logo and domain don't infringe on others, and can be protected.

## Clearance search (preliminary, not a legal opinion)
1. **Exact and similar names** (spelling variants, sound-alikes, translations, Hebrew transliteration) in the relevant goods and services classes (software is usually Nice class 9 and 42, plus others depending on the product).
2. Search the official databases (via web search for their current search tools):
   - USPTO trademark search (US)
   - EUIPO eSearch / TMview (EU and many national offices)
   - WIPO Global Brand Database (international)
   - The Israel Patent Office trademark database (Israel)
3. **Market use** (unregistered marks can have rights too): Google, App Store and Google Play, GitHub, Product Hunt, domain registrations and social handles.
4. Rate the risk: an identical or similar name in the same or a related field = HIGH; similar name in an unrelated field = MEDIUM/LOW; a descriptive or generic name = hard to protect.

## Other checks
- Using other companies' marks: in comparative marketing, factual references only. Don't imply endorsement, and follow brand guidelines for their logos.
- Domain names that contain others' trademarks (cybersquatting risk).
- The logo shouldn't resemble existing logos in the field.

## Output
Name candidates table: name · conflicts found (with links) · risk · protectability · recommendation. Recommend a formal clearance by a trademark attorney before significant investment in a brand.
