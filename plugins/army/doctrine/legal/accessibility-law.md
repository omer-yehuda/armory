# Accessibility law

Goal: know which accessibility obligations apply legally, and what is required to meet them. Technical WCAG testing is done by `ux/accessibility`.

Verify the current versions and scope with WebSearch: standards and deadlines get updated.

## Israel
- The Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities Law (1998), and the Equal Rights for Persons with Disabilities (Service Accessibility Adjustments) Regulations, 2013, apply to websites and apps that provide a service to the public, with some exemptions (for example for small businesses below revenue thresholds: verify).
- The technical standard is Israeli Standard IS 5568, which is based on WCAG at level AA (verify which WCAG version the current edition references).
- A published **accessibility statement** is required: the accessibility level, known limitations, and contact details for accessibility issues (often an accessibility coordinator).
- Accessibility lawsuits are common in Israel, and statutory damages can apply without proof of damage. This is a real business risk for consumer-facing sites.
- Accessibility "overlay" widgets do not by themselves make a site compliant.

## EU
- The European Accessibility Act (EAA) applies from 28 June 2025 to certain products and services (for example e-commerce, banking, e-books, electronic communications, passenger transport services), with an exemption for microenterprises providing services. Technical reference: EN 301 549 (WCAG-based). Check whether the product falls in scope.
- Public sector bodies are covered by the Web Accessibility Directive.

## US
- ADA Title III: courts widely treat inaccessible websites and apps of public accommodations as violations. WCAG 2.1 AA is the de facto standard referenced in settlements. Title II (state and local government) has a DOJ rule requiring WCAG 2.1 AA (verify the compliance dates). Section 508 applies to federal agencies and their vendors.
