# AI & platform compliance

Goal: AI features and use of third-party platforms comply with the law and with the providers' terms.

Verify the current application dates and obligations with WebSearch. AI regulation is moving fast.

## AI regulation
- **EU AI Act:** classify each AI feature (prohibited practices / high-risk / limited-risk transparency obligations / minimal risk). Transparency duties include telling users they are interacting with AI, and labeling AI-generated or manipulated content (deepfakes). High-risk areas include employment, education scoring, credit, critical infrastructure and biometrics. Obligations are being applied in phases, and some dates may have been changed by later EU proposals, so check the current timeline.
- **Other jurisdictions:** US state laws (for example on AI in hiring, deepfakes and chatbot disclosure), and Israeli guidance on AI and privacy. Check which apply to the target markets.
- **Privacy overlap:** personal data sent to AI APIs needs disclosure, a legal basis, and a processor agreement (see privacy).

## Provider terms
- Model and API terms of use (OpenAI, Anthropic, Google, and others): usage policies, restricted use cases, output ownership, data retention and training opt-outs, rate and attribution requirements.
- Platform terms: app store rules for AI apps (content moderation, age ratings), and social platform API terms.

## Scraping and data collection
- The website's terms of service, robots.txt, and technical measures. Personal data in scraped content brings GDPR and Israeli privacy law into play. Unauthorized access laws (such as the US CFAA) can apply to bypassing access controls.
- Using scraped content to train models raises copyright questions. Flag them.

## Output
AI features inventory · risk classification · obligations · gaps · fixes (disclosure text, labeling, human review, logging, opt-outs). Pass disclosure text to comms.
